What I Worked On

This week, I mainly worked on improving the audio in my Opening based on the feedback we received during our last client meeting. The client mentioned that the background audio should not suddenly stop when the Opening animation ends and the introduction text appears. Instead, the sound should continue playing until users enter the main map.

I agreed with this feedback because I think suddenly cutting off the audio breaks the continuity of the experience, especially when users are still reading the introduction. Therefore, I focused on solving two problems this week: which sounds should continue playing after the animation, and how to make them loop naturally without users noticing the repetition.

Choosing the Background Sounds

In my current Opening, I use five different sounds: Bell, Wind, Seagull, Car Passing, and River. When choosing the background sounds for the introduction text, I did not want to suddenly introduce a completely new sound that users had never heard during the animation. I wanted the transition from the animation to the introduction to feel consistent, so I decided to choose from the existing sounds. After comparing them, I decided to keep River and Wind.

The main reason is that Bell, Seagull, and Car Passing are relatively short sounds with noticeable characteristics. Some of them also have relatively high pitches. I think these sounds work well when they appear at specific moments during the animation, but repeatedly playing them could become tiring and distract users while they are reading. In comparison, River and Wind are more continuous environmental sounds, making them more suitable for longer background playback.

Another reason for choosing River is its connection to the content of the Opening. The first sentence of the introduction says:

"Where the St. Lawrence and Ottawa rivers meet, hundreds of islands and islets form the Hochelaga Archipelago."

Since rivers are an important part of the Hochelaga Archipelago, I think continuing the sound of flowing water while users read the introduction helps strengthen the connection between the text, the map, and the audio.

Adjusting the Sound Balance

After deciding to keep River and Wind, I started adjusting the volume balance between them.

At first, I wanted River to be the main background sound, with Wind only serving as a subtle supporting sound. Therefore, I set River's volume relatively high and kept Wind much quieter. However, during testing, I noticed that River has a very noticeable sound texture, which easily covers up Wind. Even though both sounds were playing at the same time, I could barely hear Wind. This was not exactly what I wanted, since my original idea was to use both sounds to create a richer environmental atmosphere.

Therefore, I made another adjustment by slightly lowering River's volume and increasing Wind's volume, bringing their levels closer together. I still wanted River to be the sound that users noticed first, but Wind should also be audible rather than becoming an almost unnoticeable background element. After these adjustments, I felt that the two sounds worked better together. River remained the main sound, while Wind added another layer to the overall atmosphere.

Creating a Seamless Audio Loop

Another problem I needed to solve was how to keep the sounds playing continuously.

Initially, I considered finding or creating a longer audio file that was already designed to loop. However, I realized that this approach could create two problems. First, longer audio files usually mean larger file sizes. Since our group uses GitHub to manage and share the project, I needed to consider how large audio files might affect uploading and managing the project. Second, even if I found a new audio file, it might not have the same feeling as the sounds I had already selected and tested. I did not want to replace the existing audio just to make looping possible.

Instead, I decided to experiment with looping the existing audio files. My idea was to start playing the next copy of the same audio before the previous one finished, allowing the two clips to overlap. During the overlapping section, the first clip would gradually fade out while the second one faded in. I first tested this method in Premiere Pro. I found that the beginning and ending of the River audio could overlap quite naturally. By adding fade-in and fade-out effects, the transition between the two clips became much less noticeable.

After confirming that this approach could work, I tried implementing it in code using a Crossfade Loop. This allowed River and Wind to repeat continuously, with their volumes gradually changing during each transition. After testing, I found that the result was quite close to what I wanted. The transitions between the audio clips were not very noticeable, and I could achieve continuous playback without adding new, larger audio files.

Now, when the Opening animation ends and the introduction text appears, River and Wind no longer stop suddenly. Instead, they transition into quieter background sounds and continue playing until users enter the main map.
